About this interactive edition

This digital site was conceived as an interactive layer over the original UFRJ work, with the following pedagogical aims:

Thematic navigation

Cross-referencing the four authors around topics such as fingering, ornamentation, posture, rhythm, and articulation — something difficult to achieve through linear reading.

Dictionary tooltips

Every technical or historical term is annotated: hovering the mouse opens a window with its definition and the equivalent in the original languages.

Audiovisual support

Each treatise and each piece mentioned in the texts has curated links to reference recordings on YouTube — featuring harpsichordists renowned for their excellence in each repertoire.

Fingering visualisation

The complex fingering tables of Sancta Maria and the finger pairs of Couperin are displayed as colour-coded visual sequences — facilitating memorisation and practice.

Light and dark themes

For extended reading, dark mode reduces eye strain; light mode evokes the historical parchment of the original editions.

Modernisation without additional translation

The translated text remains complete and respected. Around it, we have organised the content, created cross-references, and opened up further resources — but we have not altered the translation.

★ Editorial principle

This edition follows the ideal expressed by the translators in their closing note: to offer the contemporary reader "a closer engagement with the work and with the thought of its authors." Technology serves solely as a facilitator — never as a substitute for careful reading.
