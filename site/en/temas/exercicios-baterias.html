Exercises and Batteries

From the First Lesson to the crossed-hands batteries of Les Cyclopes

Couperin (Part 3) and Rameau (Part 3) diverge strongly on how to form the hands, but converge on the principle: before the pieces, systematic and progressive exercises. This page maps the practice routine proposed by the two French masters.

Rameau — The First Lesson

The simplest — and most profound — exercise in all harpsichord technical literature:

Five fingers on five consecutive keys

1 2 3 4 5

Right hand: C–D–E–F–G. Left hand: symmetrical, C–B–A–G–F.

Procedure

  1. The 5 fingers rest on the 5 keys. Hand motionless, fingers curved.
  2. Depress with finger 1 (or 5) without any other finger moving.
  3. Pass to its neighbour — and so on from one to the next.
  4. Critical coordination: as one key is depressed, the previous finger releases its key at the exact same moment. Lifting and playing are simultaneous.
  5. Repeat with each hand separately; then with both together, "sometimes more, sometimes fewer" notes; in every imaginable combination.

★ Why this Lesson works

  • Accustoms the hand to sustaining the fingers.
  • Calibrates the finger-to-key distance through touch.
  • Gives each finger its own independent movement.
  • Equalises forces, weights, and movements among fingers.
  • Trains contrary motion between the hands.

After mastering the Lesson, "one may learn the small Minuet in Rondeau found in the same table, having taken care to mark the fingering and to omit the ornaments."

Couperin — Progressions in All Keys

Couperin proposes systematic progressions — practised in all tonalities:

"All these progressions must be practised in all the tones and semitones of the keyboard."

For both hands.

Progression of chained trills

A highly refined exercise: chaining several consecutive trills by finger substitution on the same note — a cornerstone of the French style.

"These two numbers on the same note indicate the substitution of one finger for another, with the difference that when the first number is larger, it indicates that one must ascend immediately afterwards, and when it is smaller, on the contrary, it serves to descend." — Couperin

The experiment of two simultaneous trills

"One day, while having a young person practise them, I tried letting her play both trills at the same time with the same hand. Natural aptitude, excellent hands, and the great skill she had acquired brought her to the point of playing them very regularly."

Rolls and batteries — Rameau's virtuosity

Roll roulement

A rapid passage through conjunct notes — a scale. For longer rolls: thumb crossing.

"It is excellent, especially when sharps and flats are encountered."

Battery batterie

When the notes of the Lesson are played in leaps (disjunct notes) — a broken chord.

Batteries with crossed hands — Rameau's invention

★ Les Tourbillons — rolls with both hands

"One frequently performs the same roll with both hands, where the fingers follow one another consecutively. The hands cross over each other, but one must take great care that the sound of the first key over which one hand passes be as smoothly connected to the preceding sound as if they were being played by the fingers of the same hand."

The letters D (droite, right) and G (gauche, left) indicate which hand plays each note.

★ Les Cyclopes — two innovations of his own

  1. "The hands make between themselves the consecutive movement of two drumsticks" — rapid alternation, each hand taking one note.
  2. "The left hand crosses over the right to play the bass and the upper part alternately" — a visual and auditory tour de force.
"I believe these last batteries to be my own, at least nothing of the kind has yet appeared, and I may say, in their favour, that the eye shares the pleasure that the ear receives."
Les Cyclopes — recordings

"The performance of these different batteries and these different rolls depends above all on the flexibility of the wrist, guided by gentle and light movements, with the fixed point maintained at the elbow joint when the battery exceeds the span of the hand."

Couperin vs. Rameau — two pedagogies

Couperin — vocabulary

The exercises are autonomous compositions ("closer to passages from pieces than to arid exercises"). One gradually builds a vocabulary of intervallic formulae, in all tonalities.

Couperin's pedagogy is stylistic — forming taste, not only fingers.

Rameau — mechanics

The exercises are pure movements — a single minimalist Lesson that unfolds into rolls and batteries. One gradually builds independence, evenness, and speed.

Rameau's pedagogy is kinetic — forming the physiology first, then everything else.

★ Practical recommendation

For the modern student, both methods are complementary: Rameau's First Lesson resolves the technical foundation within a few weeks; Couperin's progressions build the stylistic vocabulary over the course of years.
