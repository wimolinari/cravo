The four treatises

Four centuries of thought on the art of keyboard playing

1565
Thomas de Sancta Maria
Libro Llamado Arte de tañer Fantasía

The oldest and most detailed description of keyboard technique. Chapters XIII to XIX: the eight conditions for playing with perfection, systematic fingering, ornaments (redouble (redobro) and quebro (Spanish ornament)) and notes inégales.

Read treatise →
1637
Girolamo Frescobaldi
To the Reader

Nine brief and fundamental instructions on how to perform toccatas, partitas, passacaglias and chaconnes — the key to the Italian stylus fantasticus. "It must not be subject to strict time".

Read treatise →
1717
François Couperin
L'Art de toucher le Clavecin

The most celebrated method of the French school. Body position, ornaments, exercises to shape the hands, eight Preludes and remarks on fingering from Books I and II. The expressive harpsichord, with aspiration and suspension.

Read treatise →
1724
Jean-Philippe Rameau
De la Mécanique des doigts sur le Clavessin

The most analytical and rational description of French keyboard technique. Independence of the fingers, the First Lesson, batteries and crossed-hand passages — including those of Les Cyclopes and Les Tourbillons.

Read treatise →

Study by theme

Cross-reference all four authors around the same subject

Body and hand position

Seat height, wrist, elbows. The "cat's hand / cat's claw" of Sancta Maria, Couperin's chair, Rameau's elbows.

Fingering

From old fingering (third over second) to modern. Complete tables from Sancta Maria, observations by Couperin, and thumb-under technique in Rameau.

Ornaments

Redouble (redobro) and quebro (Spanish ornament) (Sancta Maria), trills by Frescobaldi, mordents and appoggiaturas by Couperin, cadences by Rameau — explained visually.

Articulation and touch

Striking with the fingertip pads (Sancta Maria), modern legato (Couperin), the gentleness of Rameau and the singing affects of Frescobaldi.

Rhythm, tempo and expression

The notes inégales of Sancta Maria, the free tempo of Frescobaldi's toccatas, the cadence of Couperin, the evenness of movement in Rameau.

Exercises and batteries

Couperin's progressions (thirds, fourths, fifths…), Rameau's First Lesson, the crossed batteries of Les Cyclopes and Les Tourbillons.

About this edition

📚 Complete text preserved

This edition presents the complete Portuguese translation published by UFRJ in 2013 — all copyright belongs to the translators. The translators' explanatory notes have been converted into interactive tooltips that appear when hovering over key terms.

🎻 Audiovisual support

Each relevant concept includes, where applicable, a curated link to reference recordings on YouTube and other platforms — harpsichordists such as Skip Sempé, Christophe Rousset, Pierre Hantaï, Andreas Staier, Blandine Verlet, Jean Rondeau and Bertrand Cuiller.

🔗 Cross-references between authors

The thematic pages allow side-by-side comparison of what each author says about the same subject — useful both for comparative studies and for the student's daily practice.
